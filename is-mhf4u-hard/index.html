Is MHF4U hard? Grade 12 Advanced Functions explained

A plain-English guide for Ontario parents and students: what makes Advanced Functions challenging, where students get stuck, and how to do well.

Book Free AssessmentWhatsApp us

QueensCoaching parent guide · Updated November 2026

The short answerMHF4U is one of the more demanding Grade 12 math courses because it brings together everything from Grade 11 Functions and adds logarithms, radians and rational functions. Students who are solid on MCR3U functions, factoring and trigonometry usually manage it well with regular practice.

What is MHF4U?

MHF4U is the Ontario Grade 12 Advanced Functions course, University Preparation. Its prerequisite is Grade 11 Functions (MCR3U), or Grade 12 Mathematics for College Technology (MCT4C). It must be taken before or at the same time as Calculus and Vectors (MCV4U). It has four strands:

  • Exponential and Logarithmic Functions: logarithms, their laws, and solving exponential equations
  • Trigonometric Functions: radians, trig graphs, identities and trig equations
  • Polynomial and Rational Functions: polynomial graphs, factoring, inequalities and asymptotes
  • Characteristics of Functions: rates of change and combining functions

See the full MHF4U course page for how we coach each unit.

Why do students find MHF4U hard?

  • It assumes Grade 11 is solid. Transformations, factoring and trig ratios from MCR3U are used in every unit.
  • Logarithms are new. They work "backwards" from exponents, which feels unfamiliar at first.
  • Radians and identities. Switching from degrees to radians and proving trig identities need a new way of thinking.
  • More abstract questions. Many questions ask students to explain or prove, not just calculate.

Signs your teen needs help in MHF4U

  • They can follow examples in class but get stuck on new questions
  • Factoring or exponent laws still cause mistakes
  • Trig identities feel like guessing
  • Marks drop in the logarithms or rational functions unit

How to do well in MHF4U

  • Refresh MCR3U first, especially transformations, factoring and trig ratios.
  • Practise a little every day. Advanced Functions rewards steady practice more than long study sessions.
  • Keep a page of laws and identities and use it until it is memorized.
  • Explain each step in words. It prepares students for "show" and "prove" questions.

MHF4U and MCV4U (Calculus)

MHF4U is the foundation for Calculus and Vectors (MCV4U), which many Ontario engineering, math, science and business programs require. Weak MHF4U skills, especially in rational functions and trigonometry, are the most common reason students struggle in Calculus. See our MCV4U course page.

What parents can do

  • Ask your teen which unit felt hardest in Grade 11 Functions. That is usually where MHF4U gaps start.
  • Encourage short daily practice instead of last-minute cramming.
  • Get help at the start of the course if university programs need MHF4U or MCV4U.
  • Book a free assessment. We check the MCR3U skills MHF4U depends on and send a written plan within 24 hours.

Questions families ask

Is MHF4U harder than MCR3U?

Most students find it harder because it builds on everything in MCR3U and adds logarithms, radians and rational functions. Strong MCR3U skills make the jump much smaller.

Do I need MHF4U for university?

Many Ontario engineering, math, science and business programs require MHF4U, and it must come before or alongside MCV4U. Check each program's admission requirements.

Can I take MHF4U and MCV4U at the same time?

Ontario allows MHF4U to be taken before or at the same time as MCV4U. Schools decide on scheduling, so ask your guidance counsellor.

Can you help with MHF4U taken online or in summer school?

Yes. We follow the same Ontario curriculum whether MHF4U is taken in day school, summer school or online.
