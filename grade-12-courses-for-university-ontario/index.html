Which Grade 12 courses do I need for engineering or health sciences in Ontario?

A plain-English planning guide for Ontario families: the Grade 12 courses these programs usually ask for, the Grade 11 courses that lead to them, and when to start planning.

Book Free AssessmentWhatsApp us

QueensCoaching parent guide · Updated November 2026

The short answerMost Ontario engineering programs ask for Grade 12 English (ENG4U), Advanced Functions (MHF4U), Calculus and Vectors (MCV4U), Chemistry (SCH4U) and Physics (SPH4U). Health science, nursing and life science programs usually ask for English, Biology (SBI4U), Chemistry (SCH4U) and at least one Grade 12 math. Requirements differ by program and university, so always confirm each one before choosing courses.

How Ontario university admission works

Ontario universities usually look at two things:

  • Program prerequisites: specific Grade 12 University (U) or University/College (M) courses that a program requires, such as MCV4U for engineering.
  • An admission average: many universities calculate it from your six best Grade 12 U or M courses, including the required ones.

Grade 12 English (ENG4U) is required by almost every university program. The best place to check exact requirements is each university's admission page and the Ontario Universities' Info site (ouinfo.ca).

Health science programs vary more than engineering. Some ask for a second math course or Physics; others ask only for one math. Kinesiology and life science programs are often similar to health sciences.

Why planning has to start in Grade 10

Every Grade 12 science and math course in the tables has a Grade 11 prerequisite, and those have Grade 10 prerequisites:

  • Science: SNC2D Grade 10 Science leads to SCH3U, SPH3U and SBI3U, which lead to SCH4U, SPH4U and SBI4U.
  • Math: MPM2D Grade 10 Math leads to MCR3U, then MHF4U, then MCV4U.

If a Grade 11 course is skipped, the Grade 12 course is usually not available without making it up, for example in summer school. That is why course choices in Grade 10 and 11 matter so much.

What if your teen is not sure yet?

Many Grade 10 and 11 students do not know yet whether they want engineering, health sciences or something else. That is normal. The safest plan is to keep doors open: take SCH3U, SPH3U, SBI3U and MCR3U in Grade 11 if the timetable allows, because they lead to the Grade 12 courses most science, engineering and health programs ask for. It is much easier to drop a Grade 12 course later than to make up a missing Grade 11 prerequisite.

College programs in health, technology and the trades have their own requirements, often Grade 12 College (C) or University (U) courses such as SCH4C or MAP4C. Check each college program's admission page.

Is it the courses or the marks that matter?

Both. Having the right courses gets an application considered, and the marks in those courses often carry the most weight. Competitive programs may also look at supplementary applications. A strong foundation in Grade 11 chemistry, physics and functions usually makes the Grade 12 marks much easier to earn. What parents can do

  • Make a short list of programs your teen is curious about, and look up their prerequisites together on ouinfo.ca.
  • Check that the Grade 11 courses on the timetable lead to every Grade 12 course those programs need.
  • Talk with your teen's guidance counsellor about timing, summer school and course load.
  • Look at which math and science courses feel hardest now, and get help early, before the Grade 12 marks that count.

Questions families ask

Do I need both MHF4U and MCV4U for engineering?

For most Ontario engineering programs, yes. MCV4U is usually required, and MHF4U must be taken before or at the same time as it. Always check each program's admission requirements.

Do health science programs need Physics?

Some do and many do not. Most ask for Biology, Chemistry and at least one Grade 12 math. Check each program, since health science requirements vary widely.

Can I take a Grade 12 course in summer school or online?

Many students do. Some competitive programs look at when and how courses were taken, so check the program's admission page and talk with your guidance counsellor.
