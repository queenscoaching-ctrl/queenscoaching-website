Is SCH3U hard? Grade 11 Chemistry explained

A plain-English guide for Ontario parents and students: what makes SCH3U a step up, where students usually get stuck, and how to do well.

Book Free AssessmentWhatsApp us

QueensCoaching parent guide · Updated November 2026

The short answerSCH3U is a real step up from Grade 10 Science, mainly because it is the first course where chemistry becomes math-heavy. Most students find the Quantities in Chemical Reactions unit (the mole and stoichiometry) the hardest. With solid fractions, ratios and algebra, and steady practice, it is very manageable.

What is SCH3U?

SCH3U is the Ontario Grade 11 Chemistry course, University Preparation. Its prerequisite is Grade 10 Science (SNC2D), and it leads to Grade 12 Chemistry (SCH4U). It has five content strands, plus scientific investigation skills that run through the whole course:

  • Matter, Chemical Trends and Chemical Bonding: atomic structure, the periodic table, ionic and covalent bonds
  • Chemical Reactions: reaction types, writing and balancing equations
  • Quantities in Chemical Reactions: the mole, molar mass, stoichiometry and percentage yield
  • Solutions and Solubility: concentration, dilution, solubility, acids and bases
  • Gases and Atmospheric Chemistry: the gas laws, the ideal gas law and air quality

See the full SCH3U course page for how we coach each unit.

Why do students find SCH3U hard?

SCH3U is not hard because students are "not science people". It is hard for four practical reasons:

  • A new language. Naming compounds and writing formulas feels like learning vocabulary in a new language, and everything after depends on it.
  • Much more math. The mole, stoichiometry, concentration and the gas laws all use ratios, unit conversions, scientific notation and rearranging formulas.
  • Each unit builds on the last. If balancing equations is shaky, stoichiometry falls apart. If stoichiometry is shaky, solutions and gases feel impossible.
  • Lab reports. Many students have not written a full lab report with data, sources of error and conclusions before.

The mole unit is the turning point for most students. Getting help before or during it makes the rest of the course much smoother.

Signs your teen needs help in SCH3U

  • They understand it in class but blank on the test
  • Marks drop when the mole and stoichiometry unit starts
  • They can get an answer but cannot explain the steps
  • They spend hours on homework with little progress
  • Units and significant figures keep costing marks

Each of these usually points to one or two specific gaps, often from Grade 9 or 10 math, rather than the whole course.

How to do well in SCH3U

  • Master naming and formulas early. Short daily practice beats one long session.
  • Learn one method for the mole, such as dimensional analysis, and use it every time.
  • Write units on every number. Units catch most mistakes before the teacher does.
  • Balance equations until it is automatic. Stoichiometry depends on it.
  • Do practice tests before every unit test, under time, then review every mistake.

What parents can do

You do not need to remember chemistry to help:

  • Ask your teen to teach you one step. Explaining it shows what they really understand.
  • Look at the course outline together and mark when the mole unit starts.
  • Get help early, before marks drop, not after the first low test.
  • Book a free assessment to find the exact gaps. We check the Grade 9 and 10 skills SCH3U depends on.

Do you need SCH3U for university?

SCH3U is the prerequisite for SCH4U, and SCH4U is required by many Ontario health science, life science and engineering programs. Requirements differ by program and university, so always check each program's admission page. Our guide SCH3U vs SCH4U explains what changes in Grade 12.

Questions families ask

Is SCH3U harder than SBI3U?

Many students find SCH3U more math-heavy than Grade 11 Biology, which has more reading and memorizing. Which feels harder depends on your teen's strengths.

What is the hardest unit in SCH3U?

For most students it is Quantities in Chemical Reactions, which covers the mole and stoichiometry. It relies on fractions, ratios and unit conversions.

Can you help with SCH3U taken online or in summer school?

Yes. We follow the same Ontario curriculum whether SCH3U is taken in day school, summer school or online.

When should we start SCH3U tutoring?

At the start of the course, or at the latest before the mole unit. Students who join mid-course can still catch up before the final exam.
